\begin{abstract}
Among 601 historical LLM-generated tabular candidates from three imbalanced classification datasets, 75 (12.5\%) violate source-declared structural requirements. RC-QASyn keeps structural compliance, statistical support and dataset contribution separately auditable through provenance, schema checks, local and global TRAIN evidence, descriptive admission, fixed-budget construction and bounded integer repair. All 54 eligible repairs become structurally compliant. At matched budgets, quality-coverage selection raises distinct-feature TRAIN coverage over source order from 0.622 to 0.948 on Abalone19 and from 0.416 to 0.506 on Ecoli4, with zero exact duplicates. Better geometry does not ensure better classification: expanding the repaired page-blocks0 pool lowers logistic pure-synthetic F1 from 0.446 to 0.342 (paired conditional 95\% difference interval $[-0.135,-0.074]$). On five fresh train-only splits per dataset, curated augmentation brings no uniform benefit over real-only training, and random curation remains competitive. An exploratory addendum with six more KEEL datasets, re-implemented learned curators and four published oversamplers reproduces the coverage gain without a consistent F1 or average-precision advantage. These results establish reproducible structural and geometric effects with mixed utility outcomes, without independently establishing complete semantic validity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A generated record can be readable and numerically plausible while omitting its class, violating an integer declaration or duplicating another record. In the historical population studied here, 75 of 601 candidates violate authoritative structure, including 64 of 200 page-blocks0 records. These rates describe one generation campaign, not LLM output in general, but they motivate checking record content before interpreting resemblance or usefulness.

Three questions stay distinct: does a candidate comply with justified source declarations, how well does it resemble TRAIN locally and globally, and what does it contribute to a selected dataset and its classifier? A distant record may be legitimate, a compliant one redundant, and a diverse subset may train a worse classifier. We ask how far structural filtering, heterogeneous evidence, coverage-aware selection and bounded repair improve measurable data characteristics and classification utility compared with simple alternatives, using a controlled historical audit and a separate offline replication. The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item auditable separation of schema compliance, local/global statistical support and dataset contribution, linked to immutable candidate identities;
\item evidence-aware fixed-budget construction compared with random, source-order, farthest-first and coverage-greedy controls;
\item source-preserving bounded integer repair with controlled reevaluation;
\item an analysis of where geometry and classification agree and disagree, with paired prediction uncertainty and split-specific offline generation.
\end{enumerate}
These are integration and empirical contributions built from established components; no new optimality guarantee is claimed. Annotation experiments and validity calibrators are outside the scope.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\textit{Generation and curation.} CTGAN, TabDDPM and GReaT generate tabular data with adversarial, diffusion and language models \cite{xu2019ctgan,kotelnikov2023tabddpm,borisov2023great}, and EPIC prompts LLMs for imbalanced-class synthesis \cite{kim2024epic}. RC-QASyn instead curates an existing candidate stream. Closer work filters records after generation: CLLM keeps high-confidence, low-aleatoric rows under a classifier's learning dynamics \cite{seedat2024cllm}, ReFine suppresses local redundancy while keeping rare records \cite{jiang2025refine}, Team-then-Trim adds a three-stage quality-control pipeline \cite{zhang2026t2}, and RUBRIC ranks oversampled minority candidates by learned density-ratio realism and boundary utility \cite{yu2026rubric}. RC-QASyn differs in using model-free support evidence (TRAIN-scaled nearest-neighbour and shrinkage Mahalanobis distances), explicit reject, abstain and review outcomes, and source-declared structural checks with bounded repair. We do not claim it outperforms learned curation; the addendum compares re-implemented principles of CLLM and RUBRIC, not the original systems (Section~\ref{sec:addendum}).

\textit{Evaluation, coverage and selection.} Sample-level evaluation separates fidelity, diversity and generalization \cite{alaa2022faithful}, and density and coverage metrics define support by $k$-nearest-neighbour balls \cite{naeem2020reliable}; our fixed-radius TRAIN coverage is closely related, and our duplication rates carry no probabilistic or privacy meaning. Greedy coverage and diversity selection are well studied \cite{nemhauser1978submodular,sener2018coreset}, and a well-chosen synthetic subset can outperform the full set \cite{tavakkol2025less}; classical greedy guarantees do not automatically extend to our lexicographic selector. Because quality filtering can remove modes \cite{stevenson2026collapse}, coverage stays an explicit objective. Candidate percentiles are not calibrated in the sense of \cite{guo2017calibration}; bootstrap inference depends on its resampling design \cite{efron1979bootstrap}, Wilson intervals address small positive counts \cite{brown2001interval}, and surveys and LLM-judge studies limit what recorded critic decisions establish \cite{long2024survey,zheng2023judging}.

\textit{Validation, repair and oversampling.} Schema validation is established practice in ML pipelines \cite{breck2019validation}. Constraint-aware models enforce constraints during or after generation \cite{stoian2024realistic}, and recent work learns inter-column constraints and repairs generator output \cite{zhao2026constraint}. RC-QASyn checks only source-justified declarations and allows a narrow integer-only correction with an explicit edit budget, so it never fabricates labels or features. SMOTE interpolates minority observations \cite{chawla2002smote}; Tomek links, edited nearest neighbours \cite{batista2004study} and SMOTE-IPF \cite{saez2015smoteipf} clean after oversampling, close in spirit to support-based admission. We use imbalanced-learn, including SMOTENC \cite{lemaitre2017imbalanced}. Oversampling, random selection and class weighting answer different questions: generation, curation control and classifier training.
