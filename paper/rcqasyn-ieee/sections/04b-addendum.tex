\subsection{Exploratory addendum}
\label{sec:addendum}
These experiments were declared after the main study, in settings files committed before any score was computed, and each ran once. They address four review gaps: few datasets, the half-budget minority quota, no equal-budget B2 comparison and no published baselines. Their results stay out of the preceding tables, and all tests are exploratory, without multiplicity adjustment.

Six KEEL datasets join the original three (Fig.~\ref{fig:datasets}; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:addendum-datasets}) under the RQ6 fresh-split design, with SMOTE or SMOTENC pools of size $4b$ and $b=\min(70,\max(30,\mathrm{round}(0.5\,n_{\mathrm{min}})))$; winequality-red-4 is unavailable because its source archive is empty. vehicle0 declares all 18 features as integers, unrounded SMOTE violates this in all 1{,}400 candidates, and bounded repair recovers six because nearly every record needs more than three edits. No curator can run on vehicle0, the first fresh-split case where structural checks change the outcome.

On the eight datasets with usable pools, quality-coverage does not beat random selection from the same pool (Fig.~\ref{fig:addendum-geometry}c; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:addendum-v1-e4}). Median dataset-level F1 differences are $+.001$ (logistic regression) and $+.013$ (random forest); over 40 paired splits per classifier, wins, ties and losses are 11/22/7 and 15/15/10, and Wilcoxon $p$-values for F1 and AP range from $.19$ to $1.00$. The geometric effect replicates on all five new datasets with usable pools (Fig.~\ref{fig:addendum-geometry}a,b): on abalone9-18, coverage is $.677$ versus $.461$ and near-duplication $.000$ versus $.327$. Picking the best augmentation per dataset can appear to beat real-only training, but uses the same TEST splits and is optimistic by construction.

On the historical split (Table~\ref{tab:addendum-v1-e2e3}), a minority quota of $\lfloor b\times$ TRAIN minority fraction$\rfloor$ (0, 4 and 5 instead of 28, 35 and 24) leaves coverage unchanged. Pure TSTR F1 rises on page-blocks0 ($.446$ to $.593$ for logistic regression, $.342$ to $.631$ for random forest) as near-duplication falls from $.327$ to zero, but falls on Ecoli4 ($.296$ to $.250$, random forest) and slightly on Abalone19, and hybrid F1 moves by at most $.011$, so the half-budget quota shapes synthetic-only results in both directions but does not mainly determine hybrid utility. At equal budget, quality-coverage covers more TRAIN support than random B2-accepted, structurally complete subsets ($.948$, $.506$, $.932$ versus $.853$, $.419$, $.905$), but F1 has no consistent winner: B2 subsets lead on page-blocks0 random-forest TSTR ($.438$ versus $.342$) and trail on Ecoli4 ($.271$ versus $.296$).

The second addendum adds two curators that re-implement published principles, not the authors' code: a CLLM-style learning-dynamics filter \cite{seedat2024cllm} that keeps low-aleatoric, high-confidence candidates under a gradient-boosting model fitted on real TRAIN, and a discriminator-realism filter after RUBRIC's realism component \cite{yu2026rubric} and sample-level auditing \cite{alaa2022faithful} that keeps the candidates a random forest finds hardest to separate from real minority rows. All 45 rebuilt pools match their recorded fingerprints, and the 930 overlapping rows reproduce exactly. Among eight curators (Fig.~\ref{fig:addendum-benchmark}a; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:addendum-curators}), quality-coverage ranks best only for logistic-regression F1 ($3.75$ of 8), the CLLM-style filter for logistic-regression AP ($2.44$) and the discriminator for random-forest AP ($2.88$); Friedman tests do not separate the curators ($p=.21$ to $.65$).

Four imbalanced-learn oversamplers \cite{lemaitre2017imbalanced}, SMOTE-ENN, SMOTE-Tomek \cite{batista2004study}, Borderline-SMOTE \cite{han2005borderline} and ADASYN \cite{he2008adasyn}, run with full balancing and with $b$ added rows (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:addendum-oversamplers}). Full balancing helps random forest most on the hardest datasets (SMOTE-Tomek F1 $.433$ on yeast4 against $.268$ for quality-coverage; Fig.~\ref{fig:addendum-benchmark}c) but often lowers logistic-regression F1, to $.63$--$.68$ on yeast3 against $.78$. Borderline-SMOTE and ADASYN cannot handle nominal features, the SMOTE-ENN and SMOTE-Tomek cleaning step fails on nominal columns, and ADASYN with $b$ added rows generates nothing on page-blocks0, so the complete-case comparison covers five datasets. There, full-balancing SMOTE-Tomek and ADASYN have the best random-forest F1 ranks ($3.70$ and $4.50$ against $7.60$ for quality-coverage; Friedman $p=.031$; Fig.~\ref{fig:addendum-benchmark}b, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:addendum-ranks}), no method dominates the other three classifier--metric combinations, and no Wilcoxon comparison with quality-coverage reaches $p<.05$ (smallest $.06$). As context only, our real-only and SMOTE AUROC values exceed those of Newaz and Haq \cite{newaz2022hybrid} on six shared datasets under 10-fold cross-validation, and the SMOTE effect's sign agrees in five of twelve dataset--classifier pairs.

On nine datasets, the addenda agree with the main study: quality-coverage reliably improves the coverage and redundancy of the curated set but not classifier utility over simple selection or published oversampling.
