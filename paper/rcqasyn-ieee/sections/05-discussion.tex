\section{Discussion}
The strongest empirical contribution is a measurable separation of compliance, support, geometry and utility. Structural defects occur often enough in this historical stream to motivate explicit checks, and narrow repair corrects an eligible subset without fabricating labels. Coverage-oriented construction can reduce redundancy, but its classifier consequences remain mixed. Geometric evidence should therefore accompany direct task evaluation.

Admission ablations estimate the effect of changing candidate availability under fixed downstream rules, not a score's isolated utility effect with selected identities held constant. Repair changes both pool and selection; its utility decrease cannot be assigned solely to rounding one field. Source order can exclude the minority entirely, making unavailable TSTR a meaningful result rather than justification for adding ineligible data.

Simple alternatives are informative. Farthest-first eliminates duplicates, random selection remains competitive offline, and balanced logistic loss gives nonzero Abalone19 F1 where fixed-budget augmentation does not. The contribution is auditable curation with explicit tradeoffs rather than universally superior classifier training.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
The historical study uses three datasets, a previously evaluated TEST and recorded exact-feature TRAIN/TEST overlap. Full original TRAIN influenced generation, preventing independent split claims from that pool. The fresh study rebuilds all fitted objects and candidates, but holdouts overlap and generators differ from the LLM stream. ROS adds no new support, and minority-only pools preclude pure TSTR. Offline findings, including the nine-dataset addendum (Section~\ref{sec:addendum}), do not establish LLM-generation replication.

Four and six historical TEST positives limit Ecoli4 and Abalone19 inference, and the addendum adds datasets with only eight (abalone9-18) and ten (yeast4) positives per split. Stratified intervals condition on observed records and fixed models; they do not quantify generation/refitting uncertainty or discover unseen positive modes. Undefined MCC margins are excluded with counts reported, potentially changing the conditional target. Comparisons are exploratory, without multiplicity-adjusted significance or state-of-the-art claims.

Source declarations establish a limited contract. Physical constraints, original target meaning and cross-field semantics remain unresolved where sources cannot justify them. No human annotation experiment is conducted, so complete semantic validity is not independently established. Evidence remains uncalibrated and provisional admission is a selection policy, not verified validity. Distances depend on scaling, support and covariance assumptions; coverage establishes neither predictive accuracy nor privacy.

The utility evidence does not favour the proposed selector. Several gaps raised in review are now tested in the exploratory addendum, and none changes this reading. Across eight datasets with usable pools, quality-coverage improves coverage and redundancy but not F1 or AP relative to random selection from the same admitted pool. Setting the minority quota to the observed class ratio leaves coverage unchanged and moves synthetic-only F1 in both directions. At an equal budget, B2-accepted random subsets are neither consistently better nor worse. Published full-balancing oversamplers rank above quality-coverage for random-forest F1. Equal-budget comparisons with B1 and B3 remain unreported; B3 would require new judge calls. The learned-curation baselines re-implement the principles of CLLM and the realism component of RUBRIC rather than the authors' code, and omit CLLM's LLM generation and RUBRIC's boundary-utility term.

The addendum has its own limits. It was designed after the main results and the review were known, so it tests declared questions rather than an independent replication. Several of its datasets derive from the same KEEL sources (yeast3 and yeast4, Ecoli4 and ecoli1, Abalone19 and abalone9-18); dataset-level tests treat them as independent, which overstates the effective sample. winequality-red-4 could not be obtained. Re-scoring the canonical comparators on a different platform changes some classifier metrics by up to $.0097$, and the comparison with published AUROC values uses a different protocol and is context only.

Structural checks and bounded repair are exercised mainly on the historical LLM stream. Among the fresh pools, only vehicle0 contains structural defects. There, unrounded SMOTE interpolation breaks its integer declarations in every candidate, and the repair bound of three integer edits recovers six of 1{,}400, so the current repair policy is not suited to interpolation output. An earlier project phase produced LLM-proposed validity labels for the 120-record audit sample (27 invalid, all structural; 93 indeterminate; none confirmed valid), but these are not human ground truth and are not used here.

\subsection{Future work}
Several directions remain open. First, independent datasets with more minority TEST records, drawn from sources beyond the KEEL derivatives used here, would allow utility differences to be resolved. Second, a second LLM generation campaign under fresh splits would test whether the structural and support evidence behaves as it does on the historical stream. Third, structural rules and repair bounds could be designed for interpolation output, where integer violations affect many fields at once. Fourth, B1 and B3 could be compared at an equal budget, together with full implementations of learned curation methods. Finally, blinded human validity labels for the audit sample remain to be completed.

\section{Conclusion}
RC-QASyn makes provenance, structural compliance, statistical support, fixed-budget construction and bounded repair inspectable within the existing six-phase system. The historical audit identifies 75 structural rejects, corrects all 54 eligible records and measures changes in coverage and redundancy. Paired uncertainty and fresh train-only offline experiments strengthen the evidence while revealing limited and sometimes negative utility effects. The contribution is an empirically bounded, reproducible account of curation tradeoffs.
